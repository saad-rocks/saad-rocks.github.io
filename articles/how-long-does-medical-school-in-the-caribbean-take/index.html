How Long Does Medical School in the Caribbean Take?

  • October 7, 2026
  • 2 min read

If you are planning a career in medicine, one of the first questions is simple: how long will it take? At a Caribbean medical school, the answer depends on where you start. Students with college-level science courses can enter the Doctor of Medicine (M.D.) program directly, while high school graduates complete a preparatory program first.

Here is how the timeline works at John F. Kennedy University School of Medicine (JFK) in Willemstad, Curaçao.

The short answer

  • With pre-medical coursework: about four years for the M.D. program.
  • Straight from high school: five years in total, with the three-semester Pre-M.D. Program before the M.D. and training through MD 11.

Because JFK runs three semesters a year, students move through the program without a long summer break, and they can start in Spring (January), Summer (May) or Fall (September).

Stage 1: Pre-M.D. Program (three semesters)

The Pre-M.D. Program prepares high school graduates for medical school. Each semester lasts 16 weeks and covers the sciences that medicine builds on:

  • Pre-Med 1: Biology I, General Chemistry I, Calculus, Medical Terminology
  • Pre-Med 2: Biology II, Organic Chemistry I, Physics I, English
  • Pre-Med 3: Organic Chemistry II, Physics II, Introduction to Physiology and Anatomy

Stage 2: Basic Sciences in Curaçao (MD 1–4)

The first four semesters of the M.D. program take place on campus in Willemstad. Students study the foundations of medicine, including anatomy, histology, physiology, biochemistry, immunology, pathology, pharmacology, microbiology and neuroanatomy, and finish with physical diagnosis, medical ethics and behavioral science.

Stage 3: Review semester (MD 5)

Before clinical training, students complete a review semester with the Kaplan review course and pre-clinical preparation. This semester prepares students for the next steps in their licensing path, such as the USMLE for the United States.

Stage 4: Clinical rotations (MD 6–11)

Clinical training takes place in inpatient and outpatient settings in the United States, the United Kingdom and Nepal, with clinical rotations also available in Curaçao. The length depends on where you plan to practice:

  • 72 weeks for students preparing for the USMLE and U.S. residency
  • 84 weeks for students planning to register with the General Medical Council (GMC) in the United Kingdom

Read more in 72 or 84 weeks: clinical training for the USMLE or the UK.

After graduation

Earning the M.D. is the end of medical school, not the end of training. Graduates then prepare for the licensing examinations of the country where they want to practice and apply for residency. For the United States, that includes ECFMG Certification. See ECFMG Pathway 3 explained.

Plan your own timeline

Everyone’s starting point is different. Transfer students may receive credit for medical courses already completed, and the admissions team reviews each application individually. Request information to ask about your situation, or see the admission requirements.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the M.D. program at JFK?
The Doctor of Medicine program takes about four years: four semesters of Basic Sciences in Curaçao, a review semester, and clinical rotations.
Can I start medical school straight after high school?
Yes. High school graduates start with the Pre-M.D. Program (three semesters) and then continue into the M.D. program. The full program, through MD 11, takes five years.
How many times a year can I start?
JFK has three intakes a year, in Spring (January), Summer (May) and Fall (September).
